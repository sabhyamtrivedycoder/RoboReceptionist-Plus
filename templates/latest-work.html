Triage-Bench: Rethinking Clinical Capability in Acute Emergency Trauma Intake

Senior Clinical Lead MetroHealth Trauma Informatics Group
Dataset Specification 150 Multi-Center Trauma Scans (ESI v4)
Concordance Score 98.4% (14,760 / 15,000 pts)

EXECUTIVE ABSTRACT

Current generative multimodal models fail silently in acute emergency medicine: stochastic token generation yields variable acuity predictions, non-deterministic triage classifications, and catastrophic delays in acute arterial hemorrhage triage.

We present Triage-Bench, a clinical evaluation benchmark comprising 150 diverse emergency presentations across 5 critical trauma tracks: deep cutaneous lacerations, second/third-degree thermal burns, acute periorbital ocular trauma, compound musculoskeletal fractures, and blunt subcutaneous contusions. Robo Receptionist + achieved 98.4% concordance against board-certified attending physician consensus, with 0.0% hallucination variance and sub-850ms latency.

SECTION 01

The Capability Illusion in Emergency Department AI

Emergency medicine triage operates under strict mathematical boundaries: a misclassified ESI Level 1 (Immediate) or Level 2 (Emergent) patient faces irreversible hemodynamic deterioration if delayed by more than 10 minutes.

Commercial LLMs and vision-language APIs operate under probabilistic token sampling. When evaluated on 50 identical laceration images with varying lighting, leading foundation models displayed up to 28% intra-model variance in ESI assignment. Such non-deterministic variance is clinically impermissible in Level-1 trauma centers.
